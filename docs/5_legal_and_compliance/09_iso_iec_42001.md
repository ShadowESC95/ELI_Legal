# ISO/IEC 42001:2023

The AI management system standard named in the RAIMS use case, read alongside the EU AI Act. The
standard is about an **organisation's** management system: it is the provider (and a deployer,
if it chooses) that conforms or is certified, never the software. This document shows, for each
clause and control the use case selects, what ELI Legal gives an organisation to work with and
what the organisation still has to do itself. Each row is in the requirements register.

Clause and control titles are given as a guide; rely on the published standard.

## Clauses

| Clause | Subject | What the system contributes | What the organisation must do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **6.1.2** | AI risk assessment | A risk register of 21 risks, each with cause, event, impact, controls and stated residual risk, tied to code and tests | Own the method and criteria; repeat at planned intervals; record who decided |
| **6.1.3** | AI risk treatment | Every risk names the controls that treat it; a control without code or tests fails the register check | Approve the treatment plan; produce the statement of applicability |
| **6.1.4** | AI system impact assessment | A working form covering affected people, harms and measures, shared with the fundamental-rights and data-protection assessments | Carry it out for each deployment and keep it |
| **7.5** | Documented information | The registers, the decisions record, and a per-run evidence pack that is generated rather than written; documentation in `docs/` under version control | Control, approve, retain and distribute documents under its own procedure |
| **8.1** | Operational planning and control | One versioned workflow and one policy file; both are recorded for every run; the policy cannot switch off the core controls | Plan and control changes; keep the change assessments |
| **8.2** | AI risk assessment (performed) | As 6.1.2; monitoring flags a change of model, prompt, tool, policy or workflow for re-assessment | Perform it when the flag is raised |
| **8.3** | AI risk treatment (implemented) | As 6.1.3; controls are enforced in code, not left to procedure | Verify effectiveness in use |
| **8.4** | AI system impact assessment (performed) | As 6.1.4 | Perform and record it |
| **9.1** | Monitoring, measurement, analysis and evaluation | Monitoring computed from control evidence: failed checks, overrides, approvals without challenge, unread documents, rejected model output, open incidents, version changes | Decide what is measured, when and by whom; analyse; act |
| **10.2** | Nonconformity and corrective action | A failed check holds the work; incident-grade holds open an incident; an incident closes only with containment and reasons; a restart needs a named decision | Root-cause analysis; review the effectiveness of corrective action |

## Annex A controls

| Control | Subject | What the system contributes | What remains |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A.5** | Assessing impacts of AI systems | The impact-assessment form; the report states who is affected by its limits | The assessment itself |
| **A.6.2** | AI system life cycle: requirements, design, verification and validation, deployment, operation and monitoring, technical documentation | Requirements tied to controls and tests and checked on every commit; versions of workflow, policy, model and prompts recorded per run; a governed procedure for changing the model | Validation on representative real matters; a documented release procedure |
| **A.6.2.8** | AI system recording of event logs | A signed, chained ledger of every step, tool call, source version, model and prompt version, hold, decision and release, with per-run reconciliation | Keep the ledger and its key; retain as long as required |
| **A.7.4** | Quality of data | Facts are taken word for word; every finding is re-checked against unchanged source text; a source must declare what it is complete for | The quality of the sources chosen |
| **A.7.5** | Data provenance | Every document is hashed, with who filed it and when; every legal source is registered with owner, licence, version and retrieval date; status carries the date it was confirmed | Hold the rights to the sources |
| **A.7.2, A.7.3, A.7.6** | Data for developing the system; its acquisition and preparation | When the organisation trains its own model: only approved runs of matters allowed to teach give examples, each minimised and approved by a lawyer, special categories never kept; each model's datasheet records provenance, preparation, what was left out and the gaps; a share is kept back to verify it (A.6.2.4) and a recall test fails a model that repeats what it learnt (DG-01.47 to DG-01.51, MO-01.11) | Whether to train at all; the impact assessment (A.5) and the review of each datasheet before approval |
| **A.8** | Information for interested parties | Limits and disclaimers in every report; instructions for use | Tell affected parties what the organisation decides they must be told |
| **A.9.4** | Intended use of the AI system | Profiles fix who may use the system and what the output may say; prohibited actions are refused by policy and absent from every front end | Use it only as intended; monitor that it is |
| **A.10.2** | Allocating responsibilities | The provider and deployer responsibility matrix | Agree it in the contract |

## How it sits with the EU AI Act

| ISO/IEC 42001 | EU AI Act |
|---|---|
| 6.1.2, 6.1.3, 8.2, 8.3 | Art. 9 risk management |
| 6.1.4, 8.4, A.5 | Art. 27 fundamental-rights impact assessment |
| 7.5, A.6.2 | Art. 11 and Annex IV technical documentation; Art. 17 quality management |
| A.6.2.8 | Art. 12 record-keeping; Art. 26(6) logs kept by the deployer |
| A.7.4, A.7.5 | Art. 10 data governance |
| A.8, A.9.4 | Art. 13 instructions for use; Art. 26(1) use as instructed |
| 9.1 | Art. 72 post-market monitoring |
| 10.2 | Art. 20 corrective action; Art. 73 serious incidents |
| A.10.2 | Art. 3, 25 roles and the value chain |

Conforming to ISO/IEC 42001 does not by itself show conformity with the Act. The harmonised
standards being written under the Act are the ones that will give a presumption of conformity.
