# Professional and judicial duties

What the people who use ELI Legal owe to courts, clients and parties, and how the tool bears on
each duty. Not legal advice. Rules differ by jurisdiction and change; the user is responsible for
knowing the ones that bind them. See [Jurisdiction notes](06_jurisdiction_notes.md).

## 1. The one rule that covers most of it

**You are responsible for everything that goes out under your name.** No court or regulator
accepts "the software said so". ELI Legal is built on that rule: it prepares, shows its sources,
states what it did not check, and waits for a named person.

## 2. Verifying what it tells you

Courts have sanctioned lawyers for citing authorities that did not exist (*Mata v Avianca*,
S.D.N.Y., 2023, is the best known; in England and Wales the Divisional Court addressed the
problem in *Ayinde v London Borough of Haringey* [2025] EWHC 1383 (Admin), warning of referral to
regulators and of contempt). National court rules now say the same thing in terms. Ireland's
Practice Directions HC 142 (High Court) and CA 16 (Court of Appeal), in force from 1 September
2026, are one example in a member state: they are reported to require that material produced with
generative AI be appropriately and independently verified under human control before it is used
in proceedings, and that every authority, citation and quotation be independently verified.

**What ELI Legal's checks mean, exactly:**

| It says | It means | It does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| Citation verified | The citation was found in a source you approved | That the source is complete or correct |
| Current law verified | That source recorded no negative treatment, as at the relevant date, confirmed within the pack's time limit | That no later decision exists outside that source |
| Proposition verified | The words are the source's own, or a named person confirmed them | That the passage is the ratio, or is being applied correctly |
| Fact verified | The words appear in that document at that place | That the statement is true |

So the tool narrows what you must check and shows you where to look. It does not do your
verification for you. Open the source. Read the passage in context. That is the independent
verification a court expects, and only a person can do it.

## 3. Telling the court, the tribunal or the other side

- **Under the AI Act**, a deployer must inform people who are subject to decisions made with the
  assistance of a high-risk system (Art. 26(11)). That applies in every member state.
- **National court rules** add to it. In Ireland, for example, the same practice directions are
  reported to require disclosure, where the court
  directs, of whether generative AI was used in preparing a court document and the nature and
  extent of the use, and a declaration in affidavits and witness statements that generative AI
  was not used to generate their substantive content. Non-compliance may bring costs orders,
  documents being disregarded, strike-out, or referral to the regulator.
- **Elsewhere.** Many courts, in other member states and outside the Union, have their own rules
  or standing orders. Check the court you are in.

How the tool helps: the audit trail and evidence pack record precisely what it did on a matter,
with which model, on which documents, and what a person decided. That is the material from which
an accurate statement of "nature and extent" can be made.

What to avoid: **do not use ELI Legal, or anything else, to generate the substance of an
affidavit or witness statement.** ELI Legal has no function that drafts one. Evidence is the
witness's own.

## 4. Confidentiality and privilege

Putting client or court material into a third party's service can breach the duty of
confidentiality and put privilege at risk. ELI Legal sends nothing anywhere: the network is
denied by default and a model must run on the same machine. What remains is ordinary information
security, which is the deploying organisation's: an encrypted disk, backups, a second factor,
and care about who is given access to a matter.

Within a matter, a document is filed with a scope: all parties, the neutral only, one party in
confidence, or privileged. A mediator's shared brief leaves out anything one party gave in
confidence. Choose the scope deliberately at filing; it is the control.

A party's lawyer joins a tribunal's matter only as one party's representative (filing their side's
papers and answering holds on them, never seeing the tribunal's analysis), and an administrator has no access to
any matter.

## 5. Competence and supervision

Professional rules everywhere require competence, which now includes understanding the tools
used, and supervision of juniors and non-lawyers who use them. Before relying on ELI Legal a
user should have read the [Instructions for use](../6_manuals/08_instructions_for_use.md) and
understand section 2 above. A supervising lawyer remains responsible for a junior's use of it.

## 6. Judges, tribunal members and adjudicators

- **The decision and the reasons are yours.** The judicial function cannot be delegated. ELI
  Legal gives no view on the outcome under this profile, and its report is not a draft judgment.
- **Fair procedures.** If the report surfaces an authority or a point that no party raised, and
  you are minded to rely on it, put it to the parties first. The report lists such authorities
  separately so they cannot be missed.
- **Independence and impartiality.** The tool treats both sides' material by the same rules.
  Read both sides' source passages, not only extracts.
- **Guidance.** Judiciaries publish their own: Ireland's through the Judicial Council, for
  example, and outside the Union the judiciary of England and Wales (refreshed October 2025).
  The Council of Europe's CEPEJ charter applies across Europe. Read what applies to you.
- **Open justice and records.** Decide in advance whether the report forms part of the court
  file, and how long it is kept.
- **Telling the parties.** The AI Act requires deployers to inform people subject to decisions
  made with the assistance of a high-risk system. A court or tribunal should take a position on
  how it does this.

## 7. Arbitrators

- **The mandate is personal.** A tribunal may be assisted; it may not hand over its reasoning.
- **Due process.** An award can be challenged or refused enforcement where a party was unable to
  present its case (UNCITRAL Model Law, Articles 18 and 34(2)(a)(ii); New York Convention,
  Article V(1)(b)). Material not put to the parties is the classic risk. See section 6.
- **Disclosure and agreement.** Consider telling the parties the tool is in use and recording
  the terms in the first procedural order. Guidance exists from the Chartered Institute of
  Arbitrators (2025) and the Silicon Valley Arbitration and Mediation Center (2024); check the
  institution's rules.
- **Confidentiality of deliberations.** Keep the matter's membership to the tribunal and its
  secretary.

## 8. Mediators and conciliators

- **Confidentiality** of the mediation is protected by statute in many places (in Ireland, the
  Mediation Act 2017; in the EU, Directive 2008/52/EC) and by without-prejudice privilege.
  File each party's private material as "one party in confidence".
- **Neutrality and self-determination.** The brief gives no view on outcome and proposes no
  terms. It should not be used to evaluate the parties' positions unless they have agreed to an
  evaluative process.

## 9. Solicitors and barristers acting for a party

- The memorandum is privileged work product. Keep it so: do not circulate it outside the team.
- An assessment of the client's own case is permitted under this profile. It is an aid to your
  advice, not your advice.
- Tell the client, where your rules require it, that AI-assisted tools are used on their matter,
  and consider what that means for fees.
- Your duty not to mislead the court is engaged by anything you file. See section 2.
- Discovery and disclosure obligations attach to the underlying documents exactly as before.

## 10. Who may use it

In most jurisdictions giving legal advice to the public, or conducting litigation, is reserved to
qualified people. ELI Legal is a professional tool. It should not be offered to members of the
public as a source of advice, and its roles reflect that.

## 11. Equal treatment

A model can reproduce bias present in what it was trained on. ELI Legal limits the exposure:
facts are quoted, not paraphrased; it makes no assessment of any person's credibility or
character; and nothing a model drafts is kept unless it is tied to verified findings. The
remaining risk is selection: which facts are surfaced. Read the documents, and treat an
omission as possible.
